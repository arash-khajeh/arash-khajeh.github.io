Arash Khajeh

I build AI that speeds up scientific discovery and keeps people at the center of it.

I'm a Research Scientist in the Human-Centered AI division at Toyota Research Institute (TRI). I work on agentic research systems that leave key decisions with the researcher, on generative agent simulations of workplace wellbeing, and on privacy-preserving models for workforce analytics.

From 2021 to 2025 I was on TRI's Accelerated Materials Design and Discovery team, where I paired generative models with automated molecular dynamics to design polymer electrolytes for batteries. Before that I earned a Ph.D. in Mechanical Engineering at UC Merced, using reactive simulations to study chemistry in sliding contacts.

Selected work

Human-centered AI · 2025–present

A research agent that keeps the researcher in charge

A semi-autonomous system that searches the literature, generates hypotheses, designs and builds ML pipelines, and writes up findings. It is built to work alongside researchers rather than replace them.

HHAI 2026 · first & corresponding author

Generative agent-based simulations of workforce wellbeing

An LLM writes each simulated employee's monthly reasoning and state changes, kept within human-designed limits: a wellbeing ontology built from established survey instruments, with monthly change bounds calibrated to UKHLS and MIDUS panel data. That lets us replay workplace shocks under different interventions. After an unfair promotion decision, pairing structural changes with transparent communication cut the share of high-risk agents from 35% to 6%.

Digital Discovery · 2025 · corresponding author

A self-improving loop for discovering polymer electrolytes

A conditional generative model proposes polymers, automated molecular dynamics simulations evaluate them, and the results feed the next round. In two cycles the loop found polymers with ionic conductivity above PEO, the standard reference material.

Paper Blog post

npj Computational Materials · 2024 · corresponding author

GPT and diffusion models for de novo polymer electrolytes

A comparison of GPT-based and diffusion-based generative models for designing solid polymer electrolytes, scored on validity, novelty, diversity and synthesizability. MD simulations of the generated polymers show a clear shift toward higher ionic conductivity than the training data.

Macromolecules · 2023 · first author

Predicting ion transport before the simulation finishes

Machine learning on descriptors of system behavior from early in an MD run predicts the final ion transport properties of solid polymer electrolytes, cutting the simulation time needed for each screening candidate.

Paper

Science Advances · 2019 · co-first author

Why friction spikes at atomic steps

Atomic force microscopy experiments and reactive MD simulations separate the chemical and physical contributions to friction at graphene step edges. This came out of my Ph.D. work with Prof. Ashlie Martini at UC Merced.
